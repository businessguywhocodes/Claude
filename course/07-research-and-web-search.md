# Module 7: Research and Web Search

**Track B · Everyday leverage · ~15 minutes**

## Web search

With **web search** on, Claude can look things up online and cite its sources. Use it for:

- anything recent: news, launches, regulation changes, prices
- facts you want backed by a source
- comparisons of current products, services, or vendors
- local information: opening hours, events, travel rules

Claude adds citations to its answer. **Click a couple of them.** Sources can be outdated or low quality, and occasionally a summary misreads the source.

## Research mode

**Research** is a deeper mode. Claude runs many searches, reads widely, and comes back with a structured report and citations. It takes longer (often several minutes) and is worth it for questions like:

- *"What are the main channel-manager and booking-engine options for independent hotels in India? Compare pricing models, integrations, and what reviewers complain about."*
- *"Summarise the regulatory requirements for running a homestay in Kerala, with official sources."*
- *"What has changed in Google Hotel Ads over the last 12 months, and what does it mean for small hotels?"*
- *"I'm deciding between three schools for my daughter. Research each one's curriculum, reviews, fees, and commute from [area]."*

If you connect your work tools (Module 11), Research can also look through your email, documents, and calendar alongside the web.

## Writing good research questions

Weak: *"Tell me about hotel tech."*

Strong:
> I'm preparing a 10-minute talk for hotel owners about AI in guest communication. Research:
> 1. what AI tools independent hotels are actually using for guest messaging today,
> 2. evidence (studies or credible surveys) about impact on response time, reviews, or revenue,
> 3. common failures or guest complaints.
>
> Prioritise sources from the last 18 months. Flag where evidence is weak. Output a 1-page summary followed by details with sources.

The ingredients: **purpose, specific sub-questions, time window, source quality, and output format.**

## Turning research into action

Research is a starting point. Follow up in the same chat:

- *"Turn this into a 5-slide deck outline."*
- *"Which three findings matter most for a 30-room property? Why?"*
- *"Draft an email to my team summarising this with a recommendation."*
- *"What are the weakest claims in this report? Which sources would you double-check?"*

## Try it

1. Turn on web search and ask about something that happened this month in your industry. Check two citations.
2. Run a **Research** query on a real decision you're facing, at work or at home, using the strong-question pattern above.
3. Ask Claude to critique its own report: *"Which parts of this are least reliable?"*

## Key takeaways

- Web search for current facts; Research for deep, multi-source questions.
- Always click through a few citations.
- Good research prompts specify purpose, sub-questions, time window, and format.
- Follow up to turn research into decisions and deliverables.
