## Module 1: How Claude works

**1. You ask Claude to "write a welcome email for our guests" and get something bland and generic. What's the most likely cause?**

- A. The model isn't powerful enough
- B. Claude is missing context about your property, guests and tone
- C. You need to turn on web search
- D. Claude refuses marketing tasks

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** Most weak answers come from missing context. Claude doesn't know your property, guests or voice unless you tell it, add files, or use Projects and memory.

</details>

**2. You need the current entry rules for a destination your guests are travelling to. What should you do?**

- A. Ask Claude and trust the answer
- B. Turn on web search (or give it an official source) and check the citations
- C. Use the smallest model for speed
- D. Turn on extended thinking

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** Claude's built-in knowledge has a training cutoff. For anything recent or rule-based, use web search or give it the source, then click through the citations.

</details>

**3. A chat has gone on for two hours across three different topics and answers are getting worse. Best move?**

- A. Keep going and repeat your instructions louder
- B. Ask Claude to summarise the decisions so far, then continue in a fresh chat
- C. Switch to a different app
- D. Delete your account history

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** One chat per task. Long, mixed chats fill the context window. A summary carried into a fresh chat keeps what matters and drops the noise.

</details>

**4. When does extended thinking help most?**

- A. Fixing a typo in a sentence
- B. Translating a short message
- C. Planning a complex itinerary with many constraints
- D. Writing a one-line greeting

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C.** Extended thinking lets Claude reason step by step before answering. It pays off on planning, maths and decisions with many moving parts, not on quick edits.

</details>
