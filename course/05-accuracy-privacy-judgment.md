# Module 5: Accuracy, Privacy, and Judgment

**Track A · Foundations · ~20 minutes**

Claude is a powerful tool. Like any powerful tool, you get the most from it when you know where it can go wrong.

## Accuracy: trust, but verify

### Where Claude is reliable

- Explaining concepts, structuring thinking, brainstorming
- Rewriting, editing, and changing tone
- Summarising material **you provided**
- Following a format or process you defined
- Reasoning through problems step by step

### Where to double-check

- **Specific facts**: names, dates, statistics, prices, laws, quotes
- **Recent events**: anything after its training cutoff, unless web search is on
- **Citations and links**: always click through and confirm the source says what Claude claims
- **Maths in long chains**: ask it to show its working, or to use code for calculations
- **Niche or local details**: a specific regulation in one state, a small company, a local vendor

### Habits that reduce errors

1. **Give it the source.** "Based only on the attached document..." beats relying on its memory.
2. **Ask it to flag uncertainty.** "If you're not sure about something, say so explicitly rather than guessing."
3. **Ask for quotes and sources.** Then spot-check them.
4. **Use web search or Research** for current or verifiable facts (Module 7).
5. **Ask for a self-check.** "Review your answer for errors or unsupported claims." It often catches its own mistakes.
6. **Match checking effort to stakes.** A birthday message needs no checking. A contract clause, a medical question, or a number going to investors needs a real check by you or an expert.

## Privacy: think before you paste

### General rules

- **Follow your company's AI policy.** If HotelBFF has one, it wins over anything here.
- **Use the right account.** Company work goes in the company workspace (Team/Enterprise), which has business data protections. Don't put work data in a personal account.
- **Minimise personal data.** You rarely need a guest's real name, phone number, passport, or payment details to get help with a task. Replace them with placeholders like `[GUEST NAME]`.
- **Never paste secrets.** Passwords, API keys, full card numbers, and bank details have no place in a chat.
- **Know your settings.** Check the privacy settings and understand whether chats can be used to improve models. Use incognito chats for sensitive personal topics if you prefer they're not saved.

### A quick test

Before pasting something, ask: *"Would I be comfortable if this was shared with a trusted external contractor working for us?"* If not, anonymise it or don't paste it.

## Judgment: you're still the professional

Claude drafts, analyses, and suggests. **You decide and you're accountable.**

- **Don't outsource your voice entirely.** Edit drafts so they sound like you, especially in personal messages.
- **Disclose when it matters.** Some contexts (academic work, some client deliverables, legal filings) have rules about AI assistance. Know them.
- **Be careful with people decisions.** Use Claude to structure interview questions or performance feedback, not to make hiring or firing decisions about specific people.
- **Use experts for high-stakes advice.** For health, legal, tax, and financial matters, Claude is great for understanding your situation and preparing questions. It doesn't replace a doctor, lawyer, or CA.
- **Watch for agreement bias.** If you push hard, Claude may lean toward your view. Ask it to argue the other side: "What's the strongest case against what I just said?"

## When Claude declines

Occasionally Claude will refuse or add caveats. Usually that's because the request looks harmful, deceptive, or risky out of context. If you have a legitimate need, explain the context. ("I'm writing a training guide on spotting phishing emails for our staff; show me what a typical one looks like.") If it still declines, respect that and find another route.

## Try it

1. **Catch a hallucination.** Ask Claude for 5 statistics about the Indian hotel industry with sources, with web search **off**. Then turn web search **on** and ask again. Compare and click through the sources.
2. **Anonymise.** Take a real (work-appropriate) document and practice replacing personal details with placeholders before sharing.
3. **Argue the other side.** State a decision you're leaning toward and ask Claude for the strongest case against it.

## Key takeaways

- Trust Claude for reasoning and language. Verify specific facts, numbers, and sources.
- Give it source material and ask it to flag uncertainty.
- Follow company policy, use the right account, and minimise personal data.
- Claude assists. You decide.
